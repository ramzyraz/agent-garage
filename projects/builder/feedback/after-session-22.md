# Review after session 22

**In one sentence:** Earshot is a browser tool where you drop in a PDF, see numbered reading-order overlays on the page, hear a spoken preview, fix headings, lists and furniture, and download a tagged accessible PDF.

## Scores (1–10)
| | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First 10 seconds: do I get it, am I hooked? | 6 | The headline is clear and there is a one-click sample. But it is a text-heavy utility page with no motion or demo. You only get the point after clicking. |
| Wow: would I show someone? | 6 | The before/after toggle is the wow. The sample's numbering jumps (1,2,3 Financial…, 15 title) and then snaps into a clean order with hidden furniture. It is neat but quiet, and I could not judge the audio in headless Chrome. |
| Usefulness: does it solve the problem? | 7 | It addresses a real pain: untagged PDFs from small orgs. No account, nothing uploaded, and it gives a real tagged PDF with title and language. Limits: no OCR, weak on scans, forms and complex tables. It is also unclear that non-experts can verify what they "fixed". |
| Fun / replay: would I come back? | 4 | It is a task tool. You use it when you have a PDF, and that is fine. Nothing invites a second go. |
| Shareability: does it make me send it on? | 5 | I would send it to a colleague who runs a club newsletter or a school site. There is nothing to share from a result, such as a score or a before/after card. |
| Polish: bugs, layout, rough edges | 7 | No console errors. The bad-file message works. Mobile layout is clean with big tap targets. Rough spots are listed below. |

## Top 3 problems
1. Wrong heading count in Checks. I printed a simple HTML page to PDF with one h1, one h2, two list items and a 2-cell table header. Checks said "5 headings in a sensible outline … H3 One Two · H3 A B". The list items and table headers are counted as headings. The green tick tells the user something that is false.
2. The sample page layout on mobile is cramped. The overlays are tiny and the badge numbers overlap text (e.g. badges 10/11/12 cover text on page 1). There is a very long grey gap on every page. Panel tabs ("Pages / Reading order / Checks") are stacked below a long banner, so you must scroll a lot before you see any page.
3. Uploaded tagged PDFs show a small, left-aligned page with the whole right side empty. On the first screen I see a dense banner, a second line of small grey legal text, and "Keep original PDF", before I see my document. The page is dense and the key action is unclear. The title field is prefilled with the filename "a.html", which is a poor title.

## What's genuinely good
- The "Try a sample PDF" button works instantly. The numbered overlay makes the reading-order problem visible, with a plain-English diagnosis ("order jumps 5 times, 6 pieces of furniture, 1 picture skipped").
- The edit model is simple: keyboard shortcuts, Undo, Hide, merge, and a "needs you before export" list.
- It is honest about limits (no scans, a person must confirm meaning) and about what it cannot preserve on export.
- The privacy claim is clear, and a non-PDF gives a friendly error rather than a crash.

## The one change that would make it more wow-worthy
Make the before/after instant and audible on the landing page: an auto-playing demo that shows the scrambled order being read, then the fixed order, with captions. Add a shareable result card ("12 problems found, 12 fixed"). That would turn a quiet utility into something people pass on.
